1. Spatial Distribution: From Statewide Hotspots to Urban Micro-Habitats

Comparing macro-level biodiversity zones across Victoria with high-resolution urban grid density and individual monthly sightings.

Statewide Biodiversity Corridors

Across Victoria, wildlife observations cluster heavily around Greater Melbourne, the Dandenong Ranges, and the Ballarat-Bendigo corridor. Using the Minimum Sightings slider reveals that zones within 45 km of Melbourne consistently support all 8 target species.

2. The Urban Sprawl Gradient: Concrete Adapters vs. Bushland Specialists

How distance from Melbourne's Central Business District (km_cbd) filters wildlife communities across concentric suburban rings.

Ridgeline Density Profile: Indian Mynas (median 12.2 km), Noisy Miners (17.7 km), and Rainbow Lorikeets (17.7 km) peak sharply in the inner-to-middle suburbs, whereas Australian King-Parrots (median 36.7 km) peak at the bushland fringe.

Concentric Ring Composition: Invasive birds claim their highest proportional share in the Inner Urban (<10 km) and Middle Suburbs (10-25 km) zones, while reptiles and mammals expand their share in the Outer Fringe and Regional Victoria.

3. Temporal Partitioning: The 24-Hour Clock, Seasonal Cycles & Multi-Year Growth

Wildlife avoid direct competition by partitioning the 24-hour day and responding to distinct seasonal temperature and breeding cues.

The 24-Hour Shift: Native birds surge at dawn (8 AM peak: 4,309 sightings), cold-blooded Garden Sunskinks emerge during warm solar noon hours (11 AM - 3 PM), and nocturnal Common Brushtail Possums dominate from 7 PM to 4 AM.

Seasonal Activity Index: Comparing each species against its own monthly average reveals a sharp winter brumation drop for ectothermic Garden Sunskinks in June-July (0.19× typical), followed by aSeptember spring surge across all bird species.

The Citizen Science Boom (2020-2026)

Annual wildlife reporting across Victoria expanded nearly five-fold from 2,576 sightings in 2020 to 12,237 sightings in 2025.

Interactive Tip: Click any species name in the legend of the streamgraph to isolate its multi-year trajectory. Notice how aggressive urban nectar-feeders (Rainbow Lorikeet and Noisy Miner) and introduced Indian Mynas have grown rapidly alongside expanding suburban development.
